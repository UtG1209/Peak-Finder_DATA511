# 3D dashboard prototype: "Hike Almanac" (`3d-dashboard.html`)

1. **Metaphor.** A round almanac table for one hike. In the middle stands the mountain, with the real trail and a hiker on it (Where, On the trail). Around it runs a ring of 12 months (When). Outside the ring, at each quarter, stands a cairn of stacked stones for the cost (How much). A sun crosses an arc overhead, and a dashed arc comes in from Seattle. The ring and the cairns line up by quarter, so the Q4 cairn stands behind the Oct to Dec months.
2. **Encodings.** Terrain and trail come from the real profile (332 points). The plan shape and the rock are illustrative, and height is exaggerated about 1.3 times. Checkpoint flags show Tobler clock times going up and coming back. Each month column's height is the typical high, and a glass sleeve shows the middle half of past days. The cap colour says inside or outside the limit. One figure stands for each tenth of the busiest month's crowds. Rain drops and cloud size follow wet days, flakes follow snow days and haze puffs follow smoky days. Amber labels (with an icon and words) mark heat days at 100°F or more. A glass band at 55 to 85°F shows the limit. Floor sectors are the quarters and carry the match %. Cairn stones are transport, lodging, food, entry and a hatched 15% buffer, with the total on top. The sun moves with the time slider, and the light and shadows move with it.
3. **Interaction.** Hovering anything opens a frosted card that follows the pointer, and every card names its source. A click pins a richer card (top right) and flies the camera to the object. Esc or Close unpins. Reset view flies home. The what-if controls (travellers, nights, fly or drive) animate the cairns. The time slider and Back/Forward walk the hiker. Clicking a month, quarter, cost stone or checkpoint also moves the selection.
4. **Linked-state rule.** One selection (quarter → month → checkpoint/time) lives in `PF.state`. The quarter chips, month buttons, 3D objects, 2D profile sparkline, headline and text tables all read it. Picking a quarter selects its middle month. The month sets the start time (sunrise + 1 h), the sun arc and the checkpoint times.
5. **Keyboard and fallbacks.** An "Objects in the scene" list gives one button per object: Enter pins its card, moves focus there and flies the camera to it, and Esc returns focus to the button. With the stage focused, the arrow keys step checkpoints (left/right) and months (up/down). The `<details>` text version has tables for months, quarters, checkpoints and cost. Without WebGL, under 860 px, or if three.js fails to load, a message says so in words and the text version opens. With reduced motion, the intro build-up, the idle motion (rain, haze, flags, dashes) and the camera tweens are all instant. The camera never auto-rotates.
6. **Data and models.** The data is embedded from `app/public/data` (about 14 KB). Match, Tobler timeline and position, and trip cost are ports of `models/match.ts`, `checkpoints.ts` and `cost.ts`, and a node check against the TypeScript gave 0 differences. Q4 starts at 8:10 am and is back at 12:44 pm. Typical trip costs are Q1 $1,441, Q2 $1,564, Q3 $1,487 and Q4 $1,437. Match is 100/100/0/100, so Q4 is best (a tie goes to the cheaper quarter). `meta.fake` is true, so the footer labels the numbers illustrative.
7. **Bundle estimate for the app.** It would reuse the Explore three.js chunk (three core is about 150 KB gzip) plus OrbitControls and CSS2DRenderer (about 10 KB), and the scene code would be about 15 KB gzip. That stays within the 250 KB lazy-chunk budget if it shares the chunk with `view3d`. The prototype itself loads three 0.170 from jsDelivr, while the app uses 0.187.
8. **Tested.** Both module scripts pass `node --check`. The models match the app. In headless Chromium (Playwright, light and dark) the scene loads with no console errors. Keyboard pin, focus, Esc, what-if, checkpoint stepping, hover card and click-to-pin all work.
9. **Not tested.** Real GPUs and performance (the terrain has 48k vertices, plus soft shadows), Safari or Firefox, a real screen reader, behaviour after a resize across 860 px (the 3D does not tear down), an axe run, contrast of the CSS2D labels over the scene, and the phone layout of the page around the stage.
10. **Open questions for you.** (a) Do you want this one scene, or should the ring and cairns sit beside the 2D charts? (b) Is the match % meaningful with only the temperature limit set (Q1, Q2 and Q4 all score 100%)? Should the demo also set crowd or smoke limits? (c) Is the plan shape of the trail illustrative enough, or should we use the real route from the walk files? (d) Keep the crowd figures, or switch to a colour? (e) Is the sun arc sitting behind the scene for legibility, rather than in the true south, acceptable? Note: `docs/design/*/` is gitignored, so these files are not tracked by git.
